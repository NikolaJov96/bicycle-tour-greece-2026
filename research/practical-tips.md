# Practical Tips for Road Cycling in Greece

---

## Road Surface Quality

Greece's roads vary widely:

- **Main regional roads (B-roads):** Generally well-maintained asphalt, center stripe, passable for road bikes
- **Third-level mountain roads:** Quality ranges from excellent (recently resurfaced) to rough with patches and potholes — particularly older rural roads
- **Praised areas for tarmac quality:** Mani Peninsula ("perfect tarmac"), Pieria/Olympus road to Prionia (good surface, center stripe), Meteora monastery roads
- **Areas with mixed surfaces:** Zagori (some roads are gravel/dirt tracks between villages — always verify with Komoot surface data before going), Evrytania minor roads
- **Recommendation:** Use 28mm+ tires if possible. On confirmed-tarmac routes, 25mm is fine, but wider gives comfort and margin on rough patches

---

## Traffic

- **Rural and mountain roads (June):** Very low traffic. Mountain passes and village roads often have almost no cars
- **Coastal roads in June:** Moderate — early-season tourist traffic is building but not yet peak (July/August). Busy resorts (Kassandra in Halkidiki, main Peloponnese coastal corridors) will have more cars
- **Towns and cities:** Chaotic — avoid routing through city centers. Use bypass roads
- **Tip:** Scooters and overtaking vehicles on blind mountain bends are a specific hazard — stay visible, use a rear light, be cautious on switchbacks

---

## Heat and Riding in June

June is the last comfortable month before peak summer heat. Conditions differ sharply by elevation:

| Elevation | Typical June temp | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Sea level (coastal) | 28–34°C midday | Hot by 10am, shade scarce on coastal roads |
| 400–800m (foothills) | 23–28°C | Manageable; morning start essential |
| 900–1,400m (mountain villages) | 18–24°C | Pleasant all day; Zagori, Meteora foothills, Arcadia |
| 1,400m+ (high passes) | 12–18°C | Potentially cold on descents — pack a wind layer |

**Practical rules:**
- Start riding by 7:00–7:30am; aim to finish the hardest climbing before noon
- Greek siesta (mesimeri) runs roughly 14:00–17:00: cafés, shops, and restaurants may close — do not get caught without water or food in this window on remote roads
- Carry minimum 2 × 750ml bottles; refill at every village
- High-SPF sunscreen is essential — shade is limited on mountain roads
- If caught in afternoon heat, stop in a shaded kafeneio and wait it out

## Dogs

A notable hazard on rural Greek roads, mentioned by most cycling sources:

- **Stray dogs** are common in villages and on rural roads; most bark but do not attack
- **Sheepdogs** (on mountain roads with flocks) can be more aggressive
- **Recommended approach:** Slow down or stop; place the bike between you and the dog; do not sprint away (triggers chase instinct); squirting water from a bottle is sometimes effective
- Dogs typically lose interest once you are not moving fast
- Touring in a group of 4 is an advantage — dogs are less likely to approach a large group

---

## Navigation

- Road signs are in Greek and sometimes transliterated to Latin script — not always consistent
- GPS with **offline maps** is essential (Google Maps offline, or Komoot with downloaded region)
- Komoot is the preferred tool for route planning in Greece — strong European coverage, surface-aware (shows which roads are tarmac vs. gravel), turn-by-turn
- RideWithGPS is an alternative with a large GPX route library

---

## Bike Repair and Support

- Bike shops are found in cities (Thessaloniki, Athens, Ioannina, Kalamata, Nafplio) and some larger towns
- In remote mountain areas (Zagori, Evrytania, deep Mani), there are essentially no bike shops — carry comprehensive spares:
  - At minimum: 3 tubes, 2 tires (or tubeless kit), chain lube, chain breaker, multi-tool, spoke key
- For a group of 4 road bikes, distributing spares across riders is sensible

---

## Language and Communication

- Greek uses the Cyrillic-origin Greek alphabet; most tourist-facing signage has Latin transliteration but not always
- English is spoken in tourist areas; in remote mountain villages, older locals may speak only Greek
- Having Google Translate offline (Greek) is useful for kafeneio menus and fuel station interactions

---

## Cycling Infrastructure

Cities with dedicated bike lanes: Thessaloniki, Volos, Karditsa, Larissa, Trikala, Nafplio, Kavala — though these are mostly urban lanes and not relevant to touring routes

**Bike Friendly Hotels** is a certified network of accommodation across Greece that specifically caters to cyclists (bike storage, washing facilities, early breakfast, tool kits). See [bikehotels.travel](https://www.bikehotels.travel/en/).

## June vs. Other Months

| Month | Conditions |
|-------|-----------|
| April–May | Ideal: green, cool, no crowds, but unpredictable rain in mountains |
| **June** | **Best balance: warm but not brutal in mountains, roads not yet overwhelmed, long days** |
| July–August | Extremely hot at sea level, very crowded coastal areas, not recommended |
| September–October | Also excellent; landscapes drier/browner but cooler |
